Go Fish Costa Rica

Steve and Liisa have run Go Fish out of Tamarindo since 2010, with a TripAdvisor Travelers' Choice award every year since 2012. Their old website was a dated brochure that didn't come close. I rebuilt it as ninety-eight pages that tell one charter day from dawn to sunset, and plan a guest's whole week in one request.

The starting point

From a brochure to a booking machine.

Go Fish had fifteen years of awards, hundreds of five-star reviews and one of the biggest fleets on the coast. The old site buried all of it. Guests couldn't compare boats, see what a day actually looks like, or ask for more than one trip at a time. Drag the slider to see the difference.

The old site

  • A dated brochure layout
  • Boats listed with little to compare them by
  • Awards and reviews hard to find
  • One contact form for everything

The new site

  • A homepage that walks through one charter day, dawn to sunset
  • Every boat and adventure on its own page, with filters to find the right one

The big idea

One charter day, dawn to sunset.

Most charter sites open with a price list. This one opens on the water. The homepage scrolls through a real Go Fish day in chapters, each one answering the questions a guest actually has, in the order they have them. By the end, they know exactly what they're booking.

/charters/

The whole fleet, sorted for you

Fifteen sportfishing boats and three private catamarans. Guests filter by location, group size, budget and washroom, and sort by size, so they only see the boats that fit their group.

/charters/43-ocean.html

A page for every boat

Photos, the captain, what's included, inshore or offshore, and the fish that style targets. The page works out an estimate for their group as they fill it in, then one tap adds the boat to their trip.

  • Half, three-quarter and full day options
  • Target fish change with inshore or offshore
  • "Add to my trip" on every boat and tour
/adventures/

Seventeen adventures

Zipline, ATVs, the sunset catamaran, Rio Celeste, the estuary, surf lessons, a spa day and more. All tours Steve and Liisa have done themselves, each with its own page and bookable in the same request as the fishing.

/book.html

A four-step trip planner

Trip, when, pick, send. It asks what kind of day they want, which location is closer, their dates and their group, then points them to the boats and tours that fit. They can add another day and keep going.

/trip.html

The whole week, in one request

Everything a guest adds from any page collects in My trip. They drop a pin on where they're staying, say if they need transportation, and send the whole week at once.

  • Map pin for where they're staying
  • Asks about transportation to the boat
  • One email to Go Fish with the full itinerary
/discover/fish-seasons.html

What's biting, month by month

A season calendar so guests can plan their trip around the fish they want, plus pages on the crews and equipment, the weather, and Steve and Liisa's pledge to every guest.

/dining/ · /discover/blog.html

Where to eat, and a real blog

Ten restaurants they actually recommend, and twenty-nine blog posts with fishing reports, festivals and local tips. Pages people search for, that bring them back to the boats.

On phones

Planned from a beach chair.

Most guests plan on their phone, often already in Tamarindo. Every page is built for a thumb: the planner, the filters, the boat pages and My trip, with WhatsApp a tap away. On a laptop that tap opens WhatsApp Web; on a phone it opens the app.

Behind the scenes

The stuff you don't see.

The part guests never notice, and the part that turns a visit into a booking that actually gets answered.

Booking emails

Every request lands in the Go Fish inbox with the dates, group, picks and where they're staying already laid out. The guest gets a copy from charters@gofishcr.com.

Never yesterday's info

Every page checks it's the newest version and refreshes itself once if not, so guests never see old boat details.

Google understands it

Built-in business details tell Google exactly what Go Fish is and where it runs, plus a full sitemap, and old links from the previous site pointing to their new homes.

A map pin, free

Guests drop a pin on where they're staying and the team gets a Google Maps link. No paid map service, no monthly fee.

Same email, new domain

The site moved onto gofishcr.com with only the website records changed. Their email kept working the whole time.

Fast everywhere

Hundreds of photos resized and compressed, so pages open quickly even on hotel Wi-Fi.

How it came together

From first draft to live.

Build

I pulled every boat, tour, photo and review from the old site and built the first version of all ninety-eight pages.

Rethink

The first homepage looked like a lot of other sites, so I rebuilt it around one charter day, dawn to sunset.

Refine

Go Fish sent their change list: charter lengths, adults and kids, target fish, the map pin, their top boats. All of it went in.

Launch

Moved onto gofishcr.com with email untouched, booking emails tested end to end, and every page checked on phones.
